## Moodboard observations

Patterns worth comparing, without locking the final art direction:

1. **Architecture:** broken stone, asymmetrical ruins, weathered walls, moss and overgrown routes.
2. **Nature:** small plants, tree silhouettes, winding paths and nature reclaiming built spaces.
3. **Palette:** muted greens, faded browns, stone greys and restrained blues.
4. **Lighting:** warm lanterns against cool ambient darkness; small glows rather than large effects.
5. **Atmosphere:** mist, reflective water, quiet interiors and dusk.
6. **Characters:** small readable silhouettes, clear movement and limited animation.
7. **Companions:** simple shapes, expressive eyes, subtle glow and floating gestures.
8. **Puzzles:** connected mechanisms, gradual activation and visible world-state changes.
9. **Glyphs:** consistent symbols, progressive illumination and short readable animation loops.
10. **UI:** journal pages, restrained borders, compact icons and clear typography.

## Rights and production checklist

Before incorporating any reference image or asset into the game:

- [ ] Open the original source page, not just a repost or search result.
- [ ] Confirm the creator and exact asset being used.
- [ ] Read the licence included on the source page and inside the download.
- [ ] Record required attribution and any modification/share-alike requirements.
- [ ] For commercial-game screenshots, portfolio art and Pinterest images, obtain permission before redistribution or reuse.
- [ ] Keep inspiration references separate from production assets.
- [ ] Maintain an asset-credit file for every third-party asset used in the finished game.
