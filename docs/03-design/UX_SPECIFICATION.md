# UX Specification

## Principles

Invite curiosity, let environments guide the player without a permanent marker, make interactions clear without clutter, allow safe puzzle experimentation, and keep hints optional and in-world.

## Current behavior

- Direct eight-direction input is mapped to the isometric ground plane.
- A nearby marker and a short prompt describe available actions.
- Examine, take, activate, and item use are separate actions.
- Puzzle failures are gentle and do not consume items.
- The optional reed causeway advances the traveler stone by stone with a brief hop; a wrong choice returns them safely to shore without a timer or item cost.
- After solving the waterworks wheels, the player may explore the lower cistern's saved four-turn valve pattern and water-sharing ledger; a stair always returns them to the waterworks.
- The journal records clue IDs; Beth offers three hint levels.
- Escape opens pause. The player can resume or save and return to title.
- Camera follows, turns by quarter turns, zooms a little, and can snap rather than ease.
- Reduced motion changes Beth's bob and camera easing.

## Remaining UX work

A runtime review is still needed for controller focus, text clipping, prompt position, first-time onboarding, long-route pacing, item discovery, and the recent camera/occlusion change. The camera frames slightly above the traveler; the traveler fades while moving beneath the orchard canopy painted into the background. Foreground handling in other areas and a fuller journal presentation remain open.
